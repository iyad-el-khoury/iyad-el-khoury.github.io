### Approximating solutions to the burning of trees using GNNs
As our understanding of machine learning models improves, the mathematics community has attempted to apply these new tools to the resolution of optimisation problems.

In this project, I returned to an open problem which I learnt about during my time at the **[Combi team](https://www.lix.polytechnique.fr/combi/index.html)** of **[LIX](https://www.lix.polytechnique.fr)**, the so called burning number conjecture. The conjecture is about a graph parameter called the *burning number*, which is defined via a certain covering process on the graph. The problem of calculating the burning number of a general graph is known to be NP-Hard, it is thus reasonable to try and devise an algorithm to approximate solutions to this question. Inspired by the work done for finding **[Hamiltonian cycles in a graph](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.06523)**, we trained a graph neural network to estimate the burning number of trees. Below you may find a presentation explaining the problem along with this machine learning approach. Moreover, a Jupyter Notebook may be found on my GitHub:

**[GNNs applied to the burning problem](/assets/pdfs/Burning_Problem_using_ML.pdf)** 



### Project at CMLS supervised by Lorenzo Fantini
As part of the curriculum of the Bachelor program at l'École Polytechnique, students interested in research or wanting to deepen their knowledge of a certain subject may choose to persue a research project supervised by a professor. As I became more enthusiatic and excited to persue combinatorics, I decide to take on such a project under the guidance of professor **[Lorenzo Fantini](https://lorenzofantini.eu)** at the **[Centre Mathématique Laurent Schwartz](https://cmls.ip-paris.fr)**, or *CMLS* for short.

Professor Fantini proposed for me to study Ramsey numbers and the many tools used to understand the asymptotic growth of these numbers. We first studied the probabilstic method, an extremly powerful tool in combinatorics heavily used and popularized by Paul Erdős. I gave a general presentation of the method, through its diverse applications in algebra, graph theory and geometry, then focusing on the method in the context of Ramsey Numbers. The second part of the project was dedicated to incidence geometry, a combinatorial approach of geometry which abstracts away from the notion of distance and rather on the *incidence* properties of the geometry, what point is on which line, do all pairs of distinct lines intersect, is there always a line between any two distinct points, ect..
