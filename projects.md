### Bachelor Thesis
I completed my Bachelor Thesis at the **[Discrete Mathematics & Optimization team](https://www.tudelft.nl/ewi/over-de-faculteit/afdelingen/applied-mathematics/discrete-mathematics-optimization)** of the **[Delft Institute of Applied Mathematics](https://www.tudelft.nl/ewi/over-de-faculteit/afdelingen/applied-mathematics)** under the supervision of professor **[Anurag Bishnoi](https://anuragbishnoi.wordpress.com)**. Professor Bishnoi proposed for me to study the so called *hat guessing number* which is a parameter of a graph defined via a combinatorial game. In some sense, this problem models when can we be guaranteed to deduce a certain property to   

The ultimate goal of the project was to give a geometric strategy to the game and develop a more general understanding of these numbers. By the end of the project, we came up with such a strategy using the language of incidence geometry, in particular, a simple application of the method gave a new proof of a **[recent result](https://repository.tudelft.nl/file/File_a5a35fec-c6ec-4170-9936-9471b16804cc?preview=1)** given by Lars van der Kuil, a previous master student of Prof. Bishnoi. We are still working on futher developments of this method, which I hope to make fully available soon!

### Approximation of tree burning using GNNs <span style="font-size: 0.5em;">(**[GNNs applied to the burning problem](/assets/pdfs/Burning_Problem_using_ML.pdf)** )</span>
As our understanding of machine learning models improves, the mathematics community has attempted to apply these new tools to the resolution of optimisation problems.

In this project, I returned to an open problem which I learnt about during my time at the **[Combi team](https://www.lix.polytechnique.fr/combi/index.html)** of **[LIX](https://www.lix.polytechnique.fr)**, the so called burning number conjecture. The conjecture is about a graph parameter called the *burning number*, which is defined via a certain covering process on the graph. The problem of calculating the burning number of a general graph is known to be NP-Hard, it is thus reasonable to try and devise an algorithm to approximate solutions to this question. Inspired by the work done for finding **[Hamiltonian cycles in a graph](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.06523)**, we trained a graph neural network to estimate the burning number of trees. ### Project at CMLS supervised by Lorenzo Fantini <span style="font-size: 0.5em;">(**[Rapport](/assets/pdfs/PRL_iyad_el_khoury.pdf), [Presentation](/assets/pdfs/PRL-pp-iyad.pdf)**)</span>
As part of the curriculum of the Bachelor program at l'École Polytechnique, students interested in research or wanting to deepen their knowledge of a certain subject may choose to persue a research project supervised by a professor. As I became more enthusiatic and excited to persue combinatorics, I decide to take on such a project under the guidance of professor **[Lorenzo Fantini](https://lorenzofantini.eu)** at the **[Centre de Mathématique Laurent Schwartz](https://cmls.ip-paris.fr)**, or *CMLS* for short.

Professor Fantini proposed for me to study Ramsey numbers and the many tools used to understand the asymptotic growth of these numbers. We first studied the probabilistic method, an extremly powerful tool in combinatorics heavily used and popularized by Paul Erdős. I gave a general presentation of the method, through its diverse applications in algebra, graph theory and geometry, then focusing on the method in the context of Ramsey Numbers. The second part of the project was dedicated to incidence geometry, a combinatorial approach of geometry which abstracts away from the notion of distance and rather on the *incidence* properties of the geometry: What point is on which line?
